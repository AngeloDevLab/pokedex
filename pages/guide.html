VGC (Doubles) vs. Singles

The Competitive page and Smogon tiers used throughout this site are Singles data. VGC is a different format built around doubles — the two play quite differently.

Singles

One Pokémon per side is active at a time. This is the format Smogon's OU/UU/RU/... tiers (used on this site's Competitive page) are built around. Team size is 6, no restricted-Legendary limit beyond what each tier itself bans.

VGC (Doubles)

Two Pokémon per side are active at once, best-of-3 with Team Preview. Bring 6 Pokémon, pick 4 for each game. Matches run on a shared timer (20 minutes in the current format).

Doubles-Specific Mechanics

  • Spread moves (Earthquake, Rock Slide, Heat Wave, ...) hit both opposing Pokémon at once, at reduced power (around 75%) per target.
  • Redirection: moves and abilities like Follow Me and Rage Powder force single-target moves onto the user, protecting a fragile partner.
  • Speed control: Trick Room (slowest moves first) and Tailwind (team Speed doubled for 4 turns) can flip an entire team's game plan.
  • Intimidate and other single-target debuffs hit both opposing Pokémon when used in a double battle.
  • Protect variants (Protect, Wide Guard, Quick Guard) shield against single-target, spread, or priority moves respectively.

Teambuilding Archetypes

Hyper Offense

Maximizes damage output and Speed, with little to no defensive investment — the goal is to knock out the opponent's team before they can respond. Choice Band/Specs/Scarf are staples. Mainly a Singles-format concept; VGC's best-of-3 structure and 4-of-6 selection make pure Hyper Offense less common there, though fast, high-power cores still show up.

Balance

Mixes offensive threats with defensive and utility Pokémon (hazard control, status support, redirection). The most common archetype overall — flexible enough to adapt mid-game rather than committing fully to one game plan.

Stall

Wins through attrition: Toxic, burns, and entry hazards chip the opponent down while recovery moves (Recover, Leech Seed, Leftovers) keep the stall team's own Pokémon alive. Almost exclusively a Singles-tier concept — VGC's timer and doubles pace work against a strategy built around outlasting the opponent.

Weather Teams

A weather-setting Pokémon plus "abuser" Pokémon that get a Speed or power boost under that weather — a classic archetype in both Singles and VGC, and especially strong in doubles since more of the team benefits at once.

Rain

Drizzle sets rain automatically on switch-in. Water-type moves get a 50% boost, Fire-type moves are weakened, Thunder and Hurricane gain 100% accuracy, and Swift Swim doubles Speed for the abuser.

Example core: Pelipper + Barraskewda

Sun

Drought sets sun automatically on switch-in. Fire-type moves get a 50% boost, Water-type moves are weakened, Solar Beam skips its charge turn, and Chlorophyll doubles Speed for the abuser.

Example core: Torkoal + Venusaur

Sand

Sand Stream sets sandstorm automatically on switch-in. Rock-types get a 50% Special Defense boost, non-Rock/Ground/Steel types take chip damage each turn, and Sand Rush doubles Speed for the abuser.

Example core: Tyranitar + Excadrill

Snow

Snow Warning sets snow automatically on switch-in. Ice-types get a 50% Defense boost (Snow replaced the old Hail's chip damage in Generation 9), Blizzard gains 100% accuracy, and Slush Rush doubles Speed for the abuser.

Example core: Abomasnow + Cetitan

Current Format

Regulation Set I, Scarlet & Violet's final VGC ruleset: Doubles, Level 50, up to 2 Restricted Legendary Pokémon per team, all Mythical Pokémon banned, no two Pokémon sharing a Paldea Pokédex number, no two Pokémon holding the same item.

Since April 2026, official VGC tournaments run on Pokémon Champions (currently Regulation M-B, with Mega Evolution) rather than Scarlet/Violet. This guide — like the rest of the site — stays scoped to Scarlet/Violet, so Regulation I is shown as SV's last format rather than the actual current tournament ruleset.
